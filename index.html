02 / From interest to action

The SETUP framework.

Five practical steps for assessing readiness and planning an expansion into Johor.

S

Select your zone strategically

Match the zone and its target activities to your business needs. Companies may choose the zone that best suits them.

E

Evaluate incentives and requirements

Check which Malaysian and Singapore schemes fit your activities, investment and operating model. Conditions and compliance rules differ.

T

Take the right legal steps

Choose a business structure, identify required licences, register for tax and employee contributions, and plan for customs compliance.

U

Utilise bilateral support

Seek set-up and facilitation support through Singapore’s JS-SEZ Project Office and Malaysia’s Invest Malaysia Facilitation Centre–Johor (IMFC-J).

P

Prepare your talent and supply chain

Plan workforce training and cross-border roles, and build local supply chain partnerships.

SMF JS-SEZ Playbook, pp. 6–7.

03 / Plan with care

Incentives and tax watch-outs.

Treat incentive figures as a starting point. Confirm eligibility and current conditions with the relevant agency.

What the overview lists

  • 5% corporate tax for 10 or 15 years for qualifying activities. The overview lists capital investment excluding land of at least RM500 million, and RM1 billion in the manufacturing sector.
  • 5% personal income tax for knowledge workers.
  • Sector-specific incentives, plus grants and facilitation support from both sides.

SMF JS-SEZ Playbook, p. 3. Check scheme-specific criteria before making decisions.

Keep these on your radar

A

Workdays can affect personal tax

JS-SEZ mobility does not override domestic tax rules. Double-tax relief depends on conditions such as workdays, activities and the employer’s taxable presence.

B

Structure transactions carefully

Plan cross-border activities and transactions to manage tax exposure, transfer pricing and each scheme’s compliance requirements.

C

Keep trade data consistent

Check product classification, origin and supporting documents across both sides. Errors can affect tariffs, delay shipments or lead to penalties.

SMF JS-SEZ Playbook, pp. 15–17.

04 / Plan for people

Passes for staff and business visitors.

The right route depends on the person’s role and the work they will do in Malaysia.

Employment Pass

For approved expatriate positions

The playbook describes MIDA approval for expatriate positions in manufacturing, selected services and approved regional establishments/offices. It lists salary bands by category, but flags revised requirements effective 1 June 2026.

Check the current salary criteria and application route with ESD/MIDA before applying.

SMF JS-SEZ Playbook, p. 28.

Professional Visit Pass

For temporary professional work

The playbook describes a temporary pass for foreign professionals on behalf of an overseas company, including training, consultancy and project work. It may be granted for up to 12 months and is not extendable.

SMF JS-SEZ Playbook, pp. 28–29.

Investor Pass

For eligible business travellers

The playbook describes a multiple-entry route for new, pipeline and existing investors. Stay may be up to 12 months: six months at a time, with a possible further six months subject to justification and requirements.

SMF JS-SEZ Playbook, p. 29.

The playbook directs readers to the ESD Online System for pass information and applications.
